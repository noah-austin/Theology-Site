The New Testament does not have one technical term for "the second coming." It has three ordinary Greek nouns, each with its own picture, and it uses them interchangeably. That interchangeability turns out to matter for the rapture debate.

## The three words

### Parousia (<span lang="grc">παρουσία</span>)

Literally "presence," and so "arrival." In ordinary Greek it was the word for a king or governor arriving at a city in state; the citizens would go out to meet him and escort him in. Paul's picture in 1 Thessalonians 4:17 of believers "caught up to meet the Lord in the air" uses exactly this vocabulary (<span lang="grc">ἀπάντησις</span>, *apantēsis*, "meeting," is the term for that civic escort in Matthew 25:6 and Acts 28:15).

*Parousia* is the most common of the three: 24 occurrences in the New Testament, 17 of them for Christ's coming.

### Apokalypsis (<span lang="grc">ἀποκάλυψις</span>)

"Unveiling," "revelation." The stress is on something hidden being made visible. Christ is now present but veiled; at his *apokalypsis* the veil is removed and he is seen as he is. It is the first word of the last book of the Bible (Revelation 1:1).

### Epiphaneia (<span lang="grc">ἐπιφάνεια</span>)

"Appearing," "manifestation." In the Greek world it described a god showing up to help, or a ruler's glorious visit. It is used once of the *first* coming (2 Timothy 1:10) and otherwise of the second. It is a Pastoral Epistles word: five of its six uses are in 1–2 Timothy and Titus.

## Why it matters

Early dispensational writers sometimes argued that the words map onto two events: *parousia* for the secret coming *for* the saints (the rapture), *apokalypsis* and *epiphaneia* for the public coming *with* the saints seven years later. The occurrences do not cooperate.

- ***Parousia*** is used for the rapture (1 Thessalonians 4:15) **and** for the coming in judgment that destroys the man of lawlessness (2 Thessalonians 2:8), which on any view is the end of the tribulation. Matthew 24:27 says the *parousia* will be as visible as lightning.
- ***Apokalypsis*** is what the church waits for (1 Corinthians 1:7) **and** the event in which Christ comes "in flaming fire, inflicting vengeance" (2 Thessalonians 1:7–8). The church's hope and the world's judgment are the same unveiling.
- ***Epiphaneia*** is the "blessed hope" of believers (Titus 2:13) **and** the destruction of the lawless one (2 Thessalonians 2:8, where it stands alongside *parousia* in a single phrase: "the appearing of his coming").

Each word, then, is used both of the event believers long for and of the event the wicked dread. The vocabulary describes one coming from three angles: it is an *arrival*, an *unveiling*, and an *appearing*. This is why most careful pre-tribulationists no longer make the word-based argument and rest their case instead on imminence, Revelation 3:10, and the structure of Revelation.

<Callout variant="caution" title="What this does not prove">
  <p>That the words are interchangeable shows that the New Testament does not <em>name</em> two comings. It does not by itself show there cannot be two stages of one coming; that has to be argued from the passages, not the vocabulary. The word study closes one door, not the whole debate.</p>
</Callout>
